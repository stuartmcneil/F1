How the recommendations are worked out — method & sources

The engine. Every possible legal move from your current team is tried — single driver swaps, single constructor swaps, and (when you have two free transfers) driver+driver and driver+constructor pairs. Each resulting team must fit your cost cap. A move is scored as projected points of the new team − projected points of your current team − any -10 transfer hits over the horizon you choose. Only moves that gain more than half a point are shown, best first.

Projections. A driver's base projection is their average fantasy score per race weekend this season (avgFp in data/data.js, taken from the official F1 Fantasy Strategist previews and updated by hand; you can override it). It is then adjusted for the specific race: ×1.25 on Sprint weekends (an extra scoring session); +2 for cheap drivers and −1 for premium drivers on street circuits (chaos rewards overtakers and punishes the favourites); ×0.97 at new circuits (uncertainty); +1 at a driver's home race; and a one-off +8 for Antonelli at Monza (grid penalty = overtake points). Constructors use their season average, ×1.2 on Sprint weekends. The 2x Boost is modelled as +65% of your best driver's projection, since the boost applies to the Grand Prix only.

Strategy principles baked in (drawn from the sources below): buy in-form premiums and fund them with price-floor enablers who finish races; consistency beats ceiling because a DNF is −20; Sprint weekends are worth more to premium drivers; street circuits favour cheap drivers who gain places; never take a −10 hit for less than ~10 points of expected gain; and roll a transfer when nothing clears the bar, because 3 free next week is worth more than 1 marginal move now.

Limitations: the model doesn't see practice pace, weather, grid penalties (beyond the Monza note) or price-change trends, and prices marked est. are estimates. Check the official game before the lock-in.
